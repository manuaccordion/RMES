★ Big Picture — daily landing

Today's snapshot, with a chart of the last/next days and the totals for each of the 4 properties.

  • Booking Curve (top, full-width) — cumulative revenue by booking date for the whole year. Shows where you are in the year, where you were last year, and where you're projected to end. A 🎯 % of yearly forecast achievement chip appears next to the title — how much of the projected year-end total you've already booked.
    • OTB 2026 — orange solid, thickest, with light orange area fill below. Cumulative revenue for 2026 stays, by booking date. Stops at today (vertical dashed line).
    • Forecast — orange dashed, from today to 31/12. Projects OTB forward using the same booking-window shape that LY had between today−364 and end-of-2025. Lands at the total Forecast Revenue 2026 (same number you see in the Overview tab).
    • LY 2025 (full year) — purple solid, covering the whole year. Cumulative revenue for 2025 stays by booking date, all bookings (year closed). Shows the LY pace for the full year — so you can see at every point of the year where LY had arrived at that doy, including the LY "tail" beyond STLY.
    • STLY marker — gray ring with dot on the LY curve at today − 364d. Single point that tells you where LY had arrived at the same booking-pace point you're at today. The gap between your OTB and this marker (vertically) = how much you're up/down vs LY at the same point of the cycle.
    Read it like Duetto's "Total Year Pace": the gap between OTB and the STLY marker shows whether you're booking faster or slower than last year at this point; the gap between Forecast end-of-year and LY end-of-year shows expected growth. Hover anywhere on the chart: a vertical line and dots appear on each curve at that booking date, with a tooltip showing exact revenue values, % of yearly forecast, and the diff vs LY for that exact day.
  • Pickup chart: 7 bars showing RN per day for the 7 days before today (today−7 … today−1), today excluded — this matches the standard "pickup" definition used by other RMS (Duetto, IDeaS, …). Click a bar to drill into that day's bookings — the panel below shows only the bookings entered on the clicked day. The hero tile in the top section shows Pickup 1d by default (= what was booked yesterday).
  • Hero KPI tiles (top row, 5 tiles): Pickup (1d/7d) · Revenue 2026 (OTB) · OCC 2026 · ADR 2026 · Compset rank (7d). Each tile shows the current-year value with the STLY comparison underneath (arrow + %). When All is selected, click "by property" on any tile to see a breakdown across the 4 properties.
  • Property tiles (below): when All is selected, you see 4 mini-cards side by side with revenue OTB, OCC, ADR per property.
  • Channel share donut: outer ring = current year, inner ring = STLY. In All mode, 4 mini-donuts (one per property). The delta arrows (▲/▼) compare current share vs STLY share. Channel name normalization: raw channel manager labels are folded into canonical buckets so the YoY comparison actually matches. ① Sell Strategy — the main daily tab

📖 In 30 seconds — what this tab is for

One row per stay-date, looking forward. This is where you decide the price to load on Beddy. For every day you see: how it's booking, what the engine suggests, what is the price currently active, and a quick action to accept the suggestion or override it.

Columns, left to right:

  • Date · Event · DoW — the stay-date, the event name (if any), and the day of the week. The orange tint is only on these 3 cells: it indicates the Expedia search pressure for that day (faint = low demand, more saturated = high demand). All the other columns stay neutral so numbers are easy to read. Event names come from EVENTS_CSV in data.js — if you don't see them, data.js needs to be regenerated by Claude Code.
  • OTB to date — RN booked, OCC, ADR for that night so far.
  • Pickup Nd — net pickup over the last N days: New, Cancelled, ΔRN, ADR. Standard pickup convention (same as Duetto, IDeaS, OTA Insight): Pickup N means the N days before today (from today−N to today−1, today excluded). Examples: Pickup 1 = yesterday only · Pickup 2 = 2 days ago + yesterday · Pickup 7 = the 7 days before today. Pickup 0 is also accepted and means "what was booked today only". Note: the RMES Daily Pickup factor is the only exception — it also reads today's bookings, because it needs the most up-to-date signal for the price suggestion.
  • STLY (−364d) — what was on the books at the same point of the cycle last year (today−364, day-of-week aligned). Used to read the pace.
  • Pickup STLY 1d — pickup last year on the same booking-day (today−364).
  • Last update — the price currently active for the day. Three display modes depending on the source:
    • €X in gray, no icon → Base Price as default (no action taken yet).
    • ✓ €X in green → an RMES suggestion was accepted. Hover the cell to see when.
    • 🖋 €X in orange → a manual override is active. Hover the cell to see when it was set.
  • RMES — today's pricing engine target. Three display modes:
    • ↓ €target or ↑ €target + ✓ button → the engine suggests a new price (lower or higher). Click ✓ to accept and apply.
    • ✓ in line in green → your active price is already in tune with the engine (within ±2% of the target, or you've moved past the target in the same direction the engine wanted).
    • ⚠ next to the price (in the modal and Export Pricing) → the target hit the Floor Rate and was clamped. The engine wanted to go lower but cannot.
    Click anywhere on the cell to open the full calculation modal with the 5 factors, LMF and Event Factor breakdown.
  • Rate Shopper — Mine w/RMES · Mine · Compset, all in Expedia space, with the market rank (1 = cheapest in the compset). Note: any Expedia value < 10 € is filtered out (it's a minimum-stay number leaked into the price column, not a real rate).

Accept / Override by range — the panel above the table

Set the Range (From / To dates), then use one of the actions:

  • ✓ Accept RMES (green) — accepts each day's own suggested RMES for the whole range in one click. Every day's Last update becomes that day's suggestion in green with ✓. No price field needed. Days with no RMES (e.g. past dates) are skipped. The accept becomes the active price because it is the most recent decision, even if a manual override 🖋 existed on that day.
  • 🖋 Override (orange) — type a price in €, then click to force that single fixed price on every day in the range as a manual override. Last update becomes this price in orange with 🖋 on every day, because the override is now the most recent decision.
  • ↺ Reset — wipes Base override, manual override 🖋 and accepted ✓ on every day in the range. Last update goes back to the Base Price (black, no icon).

For a single day you can also click a Last update cell directly (override one day), or the ✓ button in the RMES cell (accept one day). Right-click or Shift+click two Last update cells to override a range inline.

How RMES decides what to suggest (the "in line" rule)

RMES always computes the target price on the structural Base Price, never on your override or your last accept. This way the engine has a stable reference and cannot drift down (or up) in a loop just because you set a low (or high) price manually. Two guard-rails are always active: the Base Price stays within ±50% of the Monthly Anchor (= true ADR LY of the base RT for that month), and nothing can ever drop below the Floor Rate.

After computing the target, RMES compares it with the price you currently have loaded (the Last update) and decides whether to insist or stay silent:

  • Insists (shows ↓ €target or ↑ €target with a ✓ button) — your active price is on the "wrong side" of the target. The market signal is still calling for the move.
  • In line (shows ✓ in line in green) — either your price is within ±2% of the target, or you have already moved past the target in the same direction the engine wanted (you lowered more than asked, or raised more than asked).

Concrete example — Firenze Suite, single day:

  • Base Price = €283 (frozen, structural)
  • the 5 factors result in a Composite multiplier of ×0.92 (market is slightly weak)
  • RMES target on Base = 283 × 0.92 = €260 (no Floor here, so the target stands). This is the engine's "true suggestion".

Now four scenarios depending on what you do:

  1. You do nothing → Last update stays at €283 (Base). RMES cell shows ↓ €260. You can click ✓ to accept it, or click the cell to see the detail.
  2. You accept the suggestion (✓) → Last update becomes ✓ €260 in green. Next time RMES is recomputed: if the market is unchanged, target is still €260 and the cell shows ✓ in line (within ±2% tolerance). If the market has changed and the new target is €270, you'll see ↑ €270.
  3. You override 🖋 with €230 → Last update becomes 🖋 €230 in orange. The RMES target stays €260 (computed on Base €283, not on your override). Since you have already lowered past the target (230 ≤ 260) and the engine wanted to go down, the cell shows ✓ in line. RMES will not insist to lower further — your decision is downstream of its suggestion.
  4. You override 🖋 with €300 → Last update becomes 🖋 €300 in orange. The target is still €260 (direction: down). Your override €300 is above the target, so RMES still wants you to lower. The cell shows ↓ €260 with a tooltip explaining "your override is above the target". You decide whether to follow or to keep your €300.

Why this design: the target depends only on the market and the structural Base Price — never on your past decisions. This avoids the "loop" risk (if RMES suggested based on your last override, after a few iterations it could drift very far). The Monthly Anchor ±50% band and the Floor Rate make sure the target stays in a sane range whatever the market does.

How to read a row at a glance. Look first at the Last update column to know the price active right now (Base Price by default, accepted ✓, or override 🖋). When both an accept and an override exist for the same day, the most recent decision wins (whichever you did last). Then look at the RMES column for the engine's verdict: either a target to consider (↓/↑) or "✓ in line" if your decision is already in tune.

② Overview — month-by-month, including forecast

📖 In 30 seconds — what this tab is for

One row per month, 16 months forward. Where you see how the year is shaping up: budget vs forecast vs OTB. The forecast is automatic — no buttons. It uses last year as a baseline, then adjusts for current pace, current OCC vs LY, and the price you have loaded for future days.

Top KPIs: YTD + Forecast Revenue, Final LY 2025 reference, Δ vs Final LY, OTB already acquired.

Monthly KPI table — column by column

  • Current OTB — RN, OCC, ADR, Revenue already on the books for that month.
  • STLY (−364d) — what was on the books exactly 364 days ago for the same stay-month. Used to read the pace.
  • Final LY 2025 — actual close last year (reference baseline for the forecast).
  • YTD + Forecast (live) — past months = actuals; current month = actuals up to yesterday + forecast for the rest; future months = forecast only.
  • Month-1st snapshot — forecast frozen on day 1 of the month, with the live Δ% vs current forecast (how well the early forecast tracked).
  • Budget — budget OCC / ADR / Revenue from the budget files.
  • KPIs for achievement — Diff (Forecast − OTB), €/day to close (time-aware), €/day pickup 7d (cur vs STLY), % Achievement vs Budget.

🔮 How the Forecast is computed (no buttons, fully automatic)

For every future month, the engine builds the forecast in two parts: forecast room-nights (how many beds will be sold) × forecast ADR (at what price).

Part 1 — Forecast Room-Nights (RN)

Starts from RN sold the same month last year (Final LY), then applies a share factor that compares current pace vs LY pace:

  • If for the same month current RN are matching LY pace (e.g. you're at 70% of LY OTB at this point in the cycle, just like last year) → share ≈ 1.00 → forecast ≈ Final LY.
  • If you are ahead of LY pace (e.g. you're already at 80% of LY OTB when last year at this point you were at 70%) → share > 1.00 → forecast above LY.
  • If you are behind → share < 1.00 → forecast below LY.
  • The share is capped between 0.70 and 1.30 to avoid wild forecasts, and the result is bounded by your physical inventory (RN × days of the month).

Part 2 — Forecast ADR (price)

The forecast ADR is composed of two slices:

  • The slice already sold (OTB) → uses the actual ADR realized on those bookings.
  • The slice still to sell (= forecastRN − OTB) → uses the RMES current reference (Last update) for each future day, averaged across the month. This means: if you accept ✓ a price or you override 🖋 a price for some days, the forecast revenue updates accordingly.

If RMES current reference is not available for a day (rare), the system falls back to LY ADR × your "ADR growth" estimate from the last 90 days.

Part 3 — Forecast Revenue = forecast RN × forecast ADR

Simple multiplication. The number you see in the "YTD + Forecast" column.

Concrete example — Firenze Suite, July 2026 (3 RT: Camera Matrimoniale Deluxe, Suite con Terrazza, Suite)

  • Final LY (July 2025 close): 182 RN, €33,357 revenue (ADR €183).
  • Currently OTB: 42 RN, €9,064 revenue.
  • Pace share for this point of the cycle: 1.11 (you're ahead of LY) → forecast RN = 182 × 1.11 ≈ 202 RN.
  • Pickup ADR for the unsold 160 RN (= 202 − 42) → averaged from "Last update" prices in July: baseRT ≈ €165, Suite Terrazza ≈ €215, Suite ≈ €180.
  • Forecast revenue ≈ €38,626 → forecast ADR ≈ €191.

What happens if you accept €500 for 10 days of July via "Accept RMES" panel: those 10 days × ~3 RN per day = 30 RN will now be priced at €500 instead of €165. Forecast revenue jumps from ~€38,600 to ~€55,300 (+€16,700). The forecast reacts immediately.

Heatmap to read how booking-date and stay-date are connected. Rows = booking month, columns = stay month. Each cell = RN booked in that month for stays in that other month.

Useful to see lead time at a glance and spot which months are "booking" right now for which future months.

④ CXLTrend — cancellations

Cancellation rate and lost revenue trend. Lets you spot anomalous channels or pricing plans (e.g. a non-refundable price plan with abnormal cancellations).

⑤ OCC by Room Type

OCC broken down by Room Type. Used to spot RTs that book early vs late and the actual price/RT mix.

⑥ Rate Shopper — competitor prices

Daily prices from the Expedia compset for each property. For each competitor set a weight (0% = excluded from the average, 100% = full weight) and an offset in € (e.g. −10 = "I want to stay €10 below this competitor"). These produce two different numbers used in two different places:

• Expedia Goal Value = weighted compset price WITH the per-competitor offsets — your desired online positioning. It is used only as the maximum cap on the Base Price (the Base Price can never exceed it; the minimum stays the Floor Rate).

• Weighted Expedia Compset = weighted compset price WITHOUT offsets (weights only) — a neutral "where am I vs the market" reference. It is used by the RMES factors (D · Online Pricing and E · Demand Expedia), where offsets must not interfere.

Sanity filter: any Expedia value below 10 € is treated as missing (it's the minimum-stay number leaked into the price column, not a real rate). Floor across the 4 properties is ≥ 100 €.

⑦ RMES — pricing engine configuration

The page where you tune the pricing engine. Switch property at the top: weights, cap, pickup thresholds, Base Price parameters and LMF are independent per property; event weights are shared across properties.

📖 In 30 seconds — how the daily price is built

For each stay-date, two layers stack up:

  1. Base Price — the structural fair price for that day (built once from history + market + the property anchor). It is the default active price unless you act.
  2. RMES suggestion — the daily target, computed as Base Price × Composite × (1 + LMF%) × Event Factor, where Composite is the weighted average of the 5 RMES factors. RMES compares the target to the price you currently have loaded and either insists (↓/↑) or shows ✓ in line.

The final price is always bounded by the Floor Rate (hard minimum) and the Base Price stays within ±50% of the Monthly Anchor (sanity rail = true ADR LY of the base RT for that month). The only mechanism that can lower the price close-in is the LMF.

Block ① — Weights of the 5 RMES factors

How much each of the 5 factors contributes to the daily adjustment. Default: 20% × 5 = 100%. The sum of weights is normalized automatically. Each factor is internally capped at ±50% on the raw signal.

A · Daily Pickup — "did anything happen for this day recently?"

What it measures. Whether the day is gaining traction now. The factor turns on only if at least one new booking confirmed for this stay-date entered in the primary window: yesterday + today (2 calendar days). If none, it widens to today and the 7 previous days (8 calendar days). If still none, the factor stays neutral (0%) — no signal, no movement.

⚠ Important — this is the only place where "today" is included in pickup. Everywhere else (the Sell Strategy "Pickup Nd" column, the Big Picture pickup chart) "Pickup N" follows the industry standard and excludes today (Pickup 1 = yesterday only, Pickup 7 = the 7 days before today). The RMES Daily Pickup factor is the exception because it needs the most up-to-date signal — what's happening right now — to suggest today's price.

How the % is picked. Once activated, the % depends on the current fill rate of the day (= OTB room-nights / total capacity). Higher fill = more push up. The scale is editable per property in Block ③ (Daily Pickup thresholds). Default scale:

Fill ≤ 20%+0%
Fill 21–50%+5%
Fill 51–70%+10%
Fill 71–90%+15%
Fill > 90%+20%

Never negative. The factor only pushes the price up. To lower the price close-in (e.g. last-minute discount for a still-empty day), the system uses the separate LMF matrix (see below). Daily Pickup and LMF are independent.

Example — Firenze Suite, 30/05/2026. Fill rate: 8 RN booked on 8 rooms = 100% > 90%. Recent pickup: 3 bookings entered in the primary window (yesterday + today). Factor activates → matches "Fill > 90% → +20%". Raw dev: +20%. Applied to composite: 0.20 × 20% = +4 percentage points of contribution.

B · Pace Trend — "are we booking faster or slower than last year?"

What it measures. The booking pace of the month of stay over the last 2 weeks, compared to the same 2 weeks last year (STLY = same time last year, day-of-week aligned). It looks at the real room-nights picked up, not the prices. The two weeks are blended: the most recent week (W4) weighs 60% and the previous week (W3) weighs 40%. Using two weeks instead of one smooths the noise on small properties (Enis Guesthouse, Condotta 16), where a single quiet week can otherwise swing the factor hard.

Formula: blended_RN = 0.60 × W4 + 0.40 × W3 for both current year and LY (W4 = last 7 days, W3 = the 7 days before that). Then pace_ratio = blended_now / blended_LY and raw_dev = pace_ratio − 1, clamped at ±50%.

Decision states:

  • 2-week pace (month): the property has fresh 2-week pace data for the target stay month. The pace ratio is applied directly to user-configured thresholds.
  • Aggregate fallback (P̄): no pace data for this property/month → use the aggregate 2-week pace of all 4 properties (same Florence market). A small P̄ badge appears in the RMES cell to flag this.
  • Annual fallback (property): not even aggregate available → use the property's annual pace as proxy. If extreme (>±15%), neutralized for safety.
  • Neutralized — no data: no pace data anywhere → factor goes n/a, weight redistributed to the other active factors.

Example — Firenze, target month June 2026. Last week (W4) picked up 8 RN, the week before (W3) 6 RN → blended now = 0.60×8 + 0.40×6 = 7.2 RN. Same 2 weeks last year: W4 7 RN, W3 5 RN → blended LY = 0.60×7 + 0.40×5 = 6.2 RN. Ratio = 7.2 / 6.2 = 1.16 → raw dev = +16%. State = "2-week pace (month)". Applied to composite: 0.20 × +16% = +3.2 percentage points.

C · Online Pricing — "am I priced too high or too low vs my visible competitors?"

What it measures. My current Expedia price vs the Weighted Expedia Compset (= weighted average of competitor prices on Expedia, with the per-competitor weights configured in Rate Shopper / RMES, but without the offset). All prices are converted to Beddy-equivalent first so the comparison is apples-to-apples.

Formula: my_BeddyEq = my_Expedia / (1 + Expedia_markup) / (1 − channel_commission) (typically ÷1.17 ÷ 0.90 for Expedia in Italy). Then raw_inverted_dev = −(my_BeddyEq / weighted_compset_BeddyEq − 1). The minus sign is intentional: if I am cheaper than the compset, the ratio is < 1 and the dev is positive (= raise me); if I am more expensive, the ratio is > 1 and the dev is negative (= lower me).

Example — Alfani, single day. My Expedia price: €350. My Beddy-eq: 350 ÷ 1.17 ÷ 0.90 = €332. Weighted Expedia Compset (Beddy-eq): €277. Ratio: 332 / 277 = 1.20. Raw inverted dev: −(1.20 − 1) = −20%. Clamped at −50%. Applied to composite: 0.20 × −20% = −4 percentage points. The market is calling me to lower.

D · Demand (Expedia) — "is search volume above or below average for this month?"

What it measures. Daily Expedia search volume for the property's destination, compared to the median of search volumes for the same calendar month. Above the median = stronger demand than usual → push up. Below = weaker demand → push down. This is the only leading automatic factor: searches move before bookings, so D can catch an unexpected demand spike (an unplanned bridge, a sudden surge) that the other factors only see once rooms are already filling.

⚠ D switches OFF on event dates. When the day has an event with a weight ≠ 0% set in the Event Factor (Block ④), the searches signal is muted for that day (it goes n/a and its weight is redistributed). Reason: an event already drives searches up, so its demand is already priced into the Event Factor — keeping D on would count the same surge twice. On dates with no event, or with an event left at 0% (label only, no price effect), D stays fully active.

Formula: raw_dev = (search_cur − month_median_search) / month_median_search. Clamped at ±50%.

Example — Firenze, 15/07/2026. Searches today: 12,500. Month-July median: 9,000. Raw dev: (12,500 − 9,000) / 9,000 = +39%. Clamped to +50% if higher. No event configured for the day → D active. Applied to composite: 0.20 × +39% = +7.8 percentage points.

E · AirDNA Market — "is the Florence market more or less booked than us right now?"

What it measures. AirDNA Booked Listings index for Florence (booked listings ÷ total market = 2,948) compared directly to MY current OCC for the same day. It is a headroom signal: if the market is fuller than us, the market is running ahead — we have margin to raise. If we are fuller than the market, we're already strong — no extra push needed. Independent from Expedia (D = search intent; E = actual market bookings).

Formula: gap = market_idx − my_OCC; then raw_dev = gap × 0.80 if |gap| ≥ 5%, otherwise 0 (deadband). Clamped at ±50%.

Deadband & gentle slope. The 5% deadband filters noise around equilibrium (so small differences don't trigger movement). The 0.80 slope (vs pure proportionality 1.00) makes the signal less aggressive — sustained gaps still drive material moves, but everyday fluctuations stay near zero.

Examples — Florence market, 20/07/2026.
(a) Headroom. Market 70% booked, my OCC 40% → gap +30% → raw dev +24% → composite contribution at 20% weight: +4.8 pp. The market is running ahead of us, room to raise.
(b) Already strong. Market 50% booked, my OCC 80% → gap −30% → raw dev −24% → contribution −4.8 pp. We're fuller than the market, no extra push.
(c) Neutral. Market 55% booked, my OCC 58% → gap −3% → inside ±5% deadband → raw dev 0. No movement on this factor.
(d) Floor case. Market 30%, my OCC 25% → gap +5% (exactly on the deadband edge) → raw dev +4% → tiny lift, +0.8 pp at 20% weight.

How the 5 dev% combine into the Composite multiplier

After each factor produces its raw dev% (clamped at ±50%, with the per-factor sanity rules above), the Composite is the weighted average:

Composite = 1 + (wA×devA + wB×devB + wC×devC + wD×devD + wE×devE)

The Composite is then capped at ±total cap (default ±30%, configurable in Block ②). So the joint signal of the 5 factors can never push the price more than ±30% on a single day.

Example with 5 factors (Pickup +20%, Pace +14%, Online −20%, Demand +39%, AirDNA +38%, all at 20% weight): Composite = 1 + (0.20×0.20 + 0.20×0.14 + 0.20×−0.20 + 0.20×0.39 + 0.20×0.38) = 1 + (0.04 + 0.028 − 0.04 + 0.078 + 0.076) = 1 + 0.182 = ×1.182. Capped to +30% if higher → final Composite = ×1.30.

⚠ When a factor is n/a — automatic weight redistribution

Some days, a factor's data is missing or unreliable. The engine then drops that factor (treats it as neutral) and redistributes its weight over the remaining active factors, so they always sum to 100%. The active factors gain more influence on that day. This is normal and visible in the modal: an n/a factor shows the strike-through weight + the reason.

Common reasons for n/a:

  • A · Daily Pickup: no new booking entered for this stay-date in the fallback window (today + 7 previous days = 8 calendar days) → factor neutral. Weight redistributed. (Common on far-future dates with no booking activity yet.)
  • B · Pace Trend: no booking pace data for the month-of-stay on this property. The engine falls back to the aggregate pace of the other 3 properties (same Florence market) as proxy — a small P̄ badge appears in the RMES cell. If even the aggregate is missing, the factor goes n/a and weight is redistributed.
  • C · Online Pricing: my Expedia price is missing for the day, or no compset visible. Factor n/a, weight redistributed. (Most frequent cause: Expedia hasn't loaded yet for that date.)
  • D · Demand (Expedia): Expedia search volume not loaded for that day, or no monthly median to compare to. Also muted on purpose on any date that has an event with a weight ≠ 0% (the event's demand is already in the Event Factor — see Block ④). Factor n/a, weight redistributed.

Concrete example. Default weights 20/20/20/20/20 (A/B/C/D/E). On a date with an event weight set, D · Demand switches off, so the active weights become 25%/25%/25%/—/25% for Pickup/Pace/Online/AirDNA. The Composite is computed only on the 4 active factors, each at 25%. You can verify it by hovering the Weight column in the modal: the strike-through 20% means "originally", and the +5% next to it means "redistributed bonus". The same redistribution happens whenever any factor goes n/a (e.g. no Expedia price → C drops, the other four share its weight).

🚨 If you see "⚠ Floor" often on the RMES suggestion

The ⚠ icon means the suggested price hit the Floor Rate and was clamped to it. The composite can pull the price low when many factors push down at once (e.g. Pace −22% + Online −50% + Demand −10% = composite ×0.79; on a €200 Base this gives €158, possibly below the €150 floor for some properties), and the Floor catches it.

If you see ⚠ Floor on many days for a property, the typical fixes are:

  • Raise the Floor Rate if you don't actually want to sell that low. The Floor is the hard minimum you accept for that property.
  • Lower the RMES total cap (Block ②) from 30% to 20% — the composite will swing less and floor hits will be rarer.
  • Review the competitors in Compset / Rate Shopper: if a competitor is selling abnormally low, it's pulling factor C down sharply. Reduce that competitor's weight or remove it.
  • Accept it: maybe the market really is weak on those days and selling at the Floor is the right move.

Block ② — RMES total cap

Final guard-rail on the Composite multiplier: ±cap% off neutral. Default = 30%. Configurable per property. Recommended range: 20–30%. Higher values let the engine react faster to market shifts but increase the risk of sharp swings.

Block ③ — Daily Pickup thresholds

The 5-row table that defines, per property, how much the Daily Pickup factor pushes when activated. Each row covers a fill-rate bucket and sets the dev %.

Edit any value and click 💾 Save thresholds. The change applies immediately to the Sell Strategy and propagates to the other browsers via the shared store. ↺ Reset to default restores the default scale (0/5/10/15/20%) for the current property only.

When to tune. If you observe that the engine pushes too aggressively on near-sold-out days for a small property (e.g. Enis Guesthouse with only 3 rooms), lower the top buckets (e.g. set the > 90% row to +10% instead of +20%). If a larger property has slow days where the engine doesn't react enough, raise the middle buckets.

Block ④ — ✨ Event Factor

For each event (loaded from EVENTS_CSV) you set a premium % from 0% to +20%. On days that match the event, the RMES price is multiplied by (1 + event%). Only positive premiums are allowed (no discount on event dates). 0% = no effect.

Every event row shows the actual dates affected (e.g. 04–10/06/2026 · 04–10/06/2027 for Calcio Storico): consecutive days are collapsed into a range, non-consecutive dates listed separately. So you can see at a glance which days the weight applies to without going to a calendar.

Weights are shared across all 4 properties: an event like "Pitti" has the same weight everywhere.

Interaction with D · Demand. Setting any weight other than 0% on a date automatically mutes the D · Demand (Expedia searches) factor for that date — its weight is redistributed to the other factors. The event already captures the demand surge, so leaving the searches signal on would double-count it. Leave an event at 0% (label only) and D stays active.

If the box shows "No events loaded": EVENTS_CSV is missing or empty in data.js. Regenerate data.js via Claude Code.

⚡ Base Price block — the structural fair price for the day

The Base Price is the structural starting price the RMES suggestion sits on top of. RMES never overwrites it — it can only suggest a target above or below.

Rolling 90-day freeze window. The Base Price is frozen only for the next 90 days from today (the window where Expedia compset data is reliable). A day is frozen with current data when it enters that window, and then stays stable. Days beyond 90 are not frozen: they recompute live each time (so they stay updatable as data improves), and get frozen automatically once they roll into the 90-day window. To refresh stale frozen values inside the window, use 🔄 Refresh frozen Base in Export Pricing (your overrides and accepts are kept).

The Base Price is computed in 4 steps, in this order:

  1. Historical ADR (LY median, base room type) — for the target date the system computes two medians and takes the higher of the two:
    • Method 1 — Same-day-LY: same day-and-month across the last two years (e.g. for 31/12/2026 → look at 31/12/2024 + 31/12/2025). Requires ≥ 2 bookings to be considered (low threshold by design: smaller properties like Enis Guesthouse have only 3 rooms, so even on special days the same-day sample is small). Catches the premium of fixed special days (NYE, 15/8, holidays) that don't show up well in a weekday average.
    • Method 2 — Weekday-and-month: same day-of-week and same month, last two years (e.g. for a Thursday in May → all Thursdays of May 2024+2025). Wider sample, smooths short-term noise. If fewer than 3 bookings on the exact weekday → widens to all days of the month.
    The system then uses max(Method 1, Method 2). If only one is available, it uses that one. The result is the median of the winning method, taking the median of the bookings (not days) in that subset — robust to one-off outliers. Every booking is counted net of OTA markup (Booking/Expedia/Airbnb divided by their markup, Direct/Beddy as-is) so all channels are compared in Beddy-equivalent. The Export Pricing tab shows both medians and which one won.
  2. × target growth — anchor multiplied by (1 + the month's target growth %). Default +5% across all months, configurable in the RMES tab.
  3. Expedia Goal Value cap (with sanity check) — the result is capped at the Goal Value (weighted compset price WITH per-competitor offsets = your desired online positioning). The Base Price can never exceed the Goal Value when the Goal Value is reliable. Reliability has 2 requirements:
    • At least 2 competitors visible on Expedia for that day. With only 1 competitor the Goal Value is statistical noise (could be a leak, an outlier, or a single offer at an unusual price), not a real online-positioning signal.
    • Goal Value ≥ Floor Rate of the property. A Goal below the property's hard minimum cannot be a sensible target — it almost always means the visible competitor prices are anomalously low (e.g. minimum-stay leaks).

    When either check fails, the cap is skipped for that day and the Base Price flows through to the Monthly Anchor guard-rail. In the Export Pricing breakdown the Goal Value cell shows ⚠ next to the price; the tooltip explains the reason ("only 1 competitor visible" or "Goal Value below Floor Rate").

  4. Monthly Anchor guard-rail (±50%) — bounded within ±50% of the Monthly Anchor, to avoid wild swings whatever the history says. See next section for how the Monthly Anchor is built.
  5. Floor — never below the property Floor Rate (the hard minimum).

📊 Monthly Anchor — the new dynamic guard-rail

The Monthly Anchor is the dynamic guard-rail of the Base Price. It is recomputed per property × per month:

  1. Take every booking confirmed in the last 2 years (e.g. for 2026 → use 2024 + 2025).
  2. Keep only stay-dates that fall in the target month (e.g. July).
  3. Keep only bookings on the base room type (Firenze: Camera Matrimoniale Deluxe · Condotta: Bilocale · Alfani: Classic · Enis: Senape).
  4. Convert every booking revenue net of OTA markup (÷1.17 Expedia, ÷1.13 Booking, ÷1.10 Airbnb; Direct/Beddy as-is) — same conversion used elsewhere.
  5. Monthly Anchor = total Beddy-eq revenue / total room-nights. This is the true ADR earned on that base RT in that month over the last 2 years.

The Base Price ±50% guard-rail then uses this Monthly Anchor as center: for example if Firenze's Monthly Anchor in July is €280, the Base Price for any July day stays within €140 – €420.

Why this design: the band of the Base Price tracks the actual seasonality of the property. High-season months get more head-room to push up, low-season months get a tighter ceiling — the Base Price becomes naturally seasonal.

Fallback if no LY data for the month: if zero bookings on the base RT existed in the target month across both LY years (e.g. a brand-new property, or a closed month), the system falls back to the annual Anchor Price configured in the RMES tab. The Export Pricing → Base Price breakdown will show the source explicitly: "Monthly Anchor (LY history, N RN)" or "Monthly Anchor (annual fallback)".

Example — Firenze Suite, 28/05/2026 (Thursday, May).

First, the Monthly Anchor for May: total Beddy-eq revenue of Camera Matrimoniale Deluxe bookings in May 2024 + May 2025, divided by total RN. Suppose result = €255 (= the ADR Firenze actually earned in May on that RT, last 2 years, net of OTA fees).

  1. LY median ADR Thursdays May across May 2024 + May 2025, base RT only, net of OTA → median = €269.
  2. Target growth May = +5% → 269 × 1.05 = €282.45.
  3. Expedia Goal Value for that date = €310 (weighted compset with offsets). 282.45 < 310 → cap not triggered. Stays €282.45.
  4. Monthly Anchor guard-rail: Anchor May = €255 → ±50% range = [127.50, 382.50]. 282.45 is inside → guard-rail not triggered.
  5. Floor Rate = €150. 282.45 > 150 → floor not triggered.

Final Base Price = €282 (rounded). This number is now frozen for 28/05/2026 and used as the reference for every RMES calculation that day, until you manually clear the freeze.

Configurable in the ⚡ Base Price block of the RMES tab:

  • Ⓑ Annual Anchor Price (fallback) — annual single number per property (Firenze 220 · Condotta 280 · Alfani 270 · Enis 145). Used as guard-rail only when LY data for the target month is missing. In normal conditions the Monthly Anchor (computed from data) is used instead.
  • Target growth % per month — applied at Step 2. Default +5% all months.
  • Floor Rate — absolute minimum. Default Firenze 150 · Condotta 200 · Alfani 200 · Enis 100.
  • OTA markup — % OTAs add to the net Beddy-equivalent price. Used in 3 places: (1) converting OTA booking revenue back to Beddy_eq in Step 1 AND in the Monthly Anchor calculation; (2) converting My Expedia → Beddy_eq for factor C · Online Pricing; (3) computing the compset references.
  • LMF matrix — Last-Minute Factor (see next section).

You can inspect the full step-by-step build-up for every day and property — including the Monthly Anchor used and its source (LY data or annual fallback) — in the Export Pricing tab → ⚡ Base Price breakdown section, and download it to CSV.

⏰ LMF — Last-Minute Factor

An additional ± % applied after the Composite, depending on (a) the current property OCC of the day and (b) the days remaining to arrival. It is the only mechanism that can lower the price close-in — Daily Pickup never goes negative, so a still-empty day at −3 days from arrival only reacts thanks to LMF.

The LMF matrix is a 9 × 4 table per property:

  • Rows = OCC buckets of the day's current occupancy: ≤10%, ≤20%, ≤30%, ≤40%, ≤50%, ≤60%, ≤70%, ≤80%, ≤100%.
  • Columns = days-to-arrival buckets: [0–10], [11–30], [31–60], [61–365].
  • Each cell holds a percentage from −20% to +20%.

Default matrix (same across all 4 properties, then editable per property):

OCC ≤0–10d11–30d31–60d61–365d
10%−15%−10%−5%0%
20%−15%−10%−5%0%
30%−10%−5%−5%0%
40%−5%−5%0%0%
50%−5%0%0%0%
60%0%0%0%0%
70%0%0%+5%+10%
80%0%+5%+10%+15%
100%0%+10%+15%+20%

How to read it. Find the row by current OCC of the day, find the column by days remaining to arrival → that's the LMF for the day. Negative = last-minute discount (low OCC, close-in arrival); positive = premium (high OCC, far-out arrival, you can charge more for inventory that's already scarce).

Example. Day is 5 days away, currently at 30% OCC → row "≤30%" × column "0–10d" → −10%. The LMF subtracts 10% from the price after the Composite. Conversely: day is 90 days away, already at 90% OCC → row "≤100%" × column "61–365d" → +20%. The LMF adds 20% to the price.

LMF is multiplicative: final_price = Base × Composite × (1 + LMF%) × Event_Factor. The final result is then clamped to the Floor Rate but has no upper cap (the Composite cap already controls upside).

Ⓒ Compset competitor weights / offsets

Same compset weights/offsets control as in Rate Shopper, available in the RMES tab too. Two outputs come from this configuration:

  • Weighted Expedia Compset (weights only, no offset) — feeds factor C · Online Pricing. Represents "where the market is" on Expedia.
  • Expedia Goal Value (weights + offsets) — caps the Base Price at step 3. Represents "where I want to be positioned" online. An offset of −10€ on a competitor means "I want to stay 10€ below this competitor".

"Apply changes" button

Changes to weights / cap / Base Price / event weights are saved only when you click "Apply changes" at the bottom of the tab. The Daily Pickup thresholds (Block ③) and the per-event weights save immediately with their own buttons. Switch property to edit the others (each property is independent for everything except event weights).

📐 Full end-to-end example — Firenze Suite, single day

  1. Base Price (step 5 above) = €283 (frozen).
  2. This day has the event "Pre Festa della Repubblica" with a weight set (+3%), so D · Demand is muted and the four active factors share the weight at 25% each:
    • A · Daily Pickup: 3 bookings entered in the primary window (yesterday + today), fill rate 100% → +20%. Weighted: 0.25 × 20% = +5 pp.
    • B · Pace Trend: 2-week blend ratio 0.92 → −8%. Weighted: 0.25 × −8% = −2 pp.
    • C · Online Pricing: my Beddy_eq vs compset → −20%. Weighted: 0.25 × −20% = −5 pp.
    • D · Demand Expedia: muted (event weight set) → weight redistributed, 0 pp.
    • E · AirDNA Market: market running ahead of us → +10%. Weighted: 0.25 × +10% = +2.5 pp.
  3. Composite raw = 1 + (5 − 2 − 5 + 2.5)/100 = 1 + 0.5/100 = ×1.005. Within ±30% cap → no trimming.
  4. LMF for today: OCC 100% × 3 days to arrival → matrix says +0% (no discount needed, nearly full). LMF factor = 1.00.
  5. Event Factor: weight +3% → Event Factor = 1.03.
  6. Final RMES target = 283 × 1.005 × 1.00 × 1.03 = €293 (rounded). Floor €150 not triggered.
  7. RMES cell shows ↑ €293. You can ✓ accept it (Last update becomes €293 green) or click it for the full detail modal.

⚑ Flag system — special-situation tags

Some days produce a Base Price that is mathematically correct but rests on data worth a second look. The flag system tags these days so you can spot them at a glance and decide whether to act (manually override) or trust the engine. Flags don't change the calculation — they are informational only.

Two severity levels:

  • 🔴 alert — action recommended: review and consider a manual Base Price override.
  • 🟡 warn — worth a glance: the number is usable but rests on weaker data.

The 4 flag types — only situations that need your attention:

  • anchor_max_strangle 🔴 alert — the Monthly Anchor MAX guard-rail cut the price by more than €30. The ADR signal for this day is much higher than the seasonal average. Typical case: NYE, Capodanno or other special days inside a low-season month. Action: if you believe the day truly sells at premium, set a manual Base Price override for that day to break free from the seasonal band.
  • lyADR_far_above_monthlyAnchor 🟡 warn — LY ADR is ≥ 2× the Monthly Anchor. A specific weekday consistently sells much higher than the rest of the month (e.g. Monday Davids in December). The Monthly Anchor band may strangle the price. Action: check whether the weekday really has a structural premium and consider overriding.
  • lyADR_far_below_monthlyAnchor 🟡 warn — LY ADR is < 40% of the Monthly Anchor. Likely data quality issue: an outlier booking pulling the median down. Action: open the booking list for that weekday and clean the data if needed.
  • small_sample_dayMatch 🟡 warn — same-day-LY won with only 2-3 bookings while the weekday-and-month method had ≥15 bookings. Premium-day signal based on a small sample, may be noisier. Action: review by hand on special-day candidates (NYE, Ferragosto, holidays) to confirm the premium is real.

Practical use: most days have no flag (clean run). When you see ⚑ on a date, hover the icon for the explanation. Address the 🔴 alerts first (typically 5-10 days a year, mostly Capodanno and similar peaks), then glance at 🟡 warns. The CSV export includes the flags column with the codes (semicolon-separated) for sorting/filtering in Excel.

Situations that DON'T trigger a flag are visible directly in the columns: ADR used "(anchor)" tag means no LY history; Goal Value "⚠" means cap disabled for sanity (n<2 competitors or Goal<Floor); Floor "✓" means Floor protected. No need for a flag to repeat what the column already shows.

🗂 Section 3 — Audit log

An append-only record of every pricing decision a person makes: accepting an RMES suggestion (✓), overriding the Base Price (🖋), or resetting either (↺). Each entry is captured at the moment of the action, together with the full engine context, so decisions can be reviewed and learned from later. The goal is to build the dataset needed to improve the suggestions over time (what did the engine propose, what did the person choose, and how far apart were they).

Local per device — not synced. Unlike the rest of the dashboard, the audit log lives only in this browser and is deliberately kept out of the shared Firebase state: an append-only log must never be overwritten by the "last write wins" sync. ☁ Shared state (real-time sync)

RMES decisions (accepted prices, overrides) and the whole configuration (the 5-factor weights, total cap, Daily Pickup thresholds, floor, anchor, target growth, LMF matrix, event weights, compset weights/offsets) are shared in real time between everyone who opens the dashboard. When one person accepts a price or changes a setting, the others see it without re-importing anything, on any browser or device.

The chip in the top bar shows the status: ☁ synced = your changes are saved to the shared store and you are seeing everyone's latest state; ⚠ local only = the connection to the shared store is unavailable, so you are working on this browser alone — everything still works and will sync automatically when the connection is back.

If two people change the same thing at the same moment, the last save wins. In practice you almost never edit the exact same date at the exact same second, so this is rarely an issue.

📚 Glossary
OTB
On The Books — bookings already confirmed for a stay-date.
RN
Room Nights = rooms × nights. E.g. 2 rooms × 3 nights = 6 RN.
OCC
Occupancy = RN sold / (rooms × days available).
ADR
Average Daily Rate = revenue / RN sold (net of city tax).
STLY
Same Time Last Year = last year's snapshot at the same point of the cycle. Day-of-week-aligned (today − 364 days = same day of week). Used to compare pace.
Final LY
The full close of last year. Different from STLY: Final LY is "what last year ended up as", STLY is "what was already on the books at this point last year".
Base Price
Structural price per stay-date, frozen, computed once. Source of the RMES suggestion.
Monthly Anchor
True ADR earned on the base RT in a given month, computed from the last 2 years of bookings (net of OTA markup). Used as guard-rail: the Base Price stays within ±50% of the Monthly Anchor. One value per property × per month.
Annual Anchor Price (fallback)
Single annual reference number per property (Firenze 220 · Condotta 280 · Alfani 270 · Enis 145). Used as guard-rail only when no LY booking data exists for the target month. Configurable in the RMES tab.
RMES delta
Today's adjustment on top of the Base Price, computed from the 5 factors (Daily Pickup, Pace Trend, Online Pricing, Demand Expedia, AirDNA Market) + LMF + Event Factor. The composite is capped at ±total cap (default 30%); the final price is clamped to the Floor Rate.
baseRT
The reference Room Type for each property (Camera Matrimoniale Deluxe for Firenze, Bilocale for Condotta, Classic for Alfani, Senape for Davids). Base Price is computed at the baseRT, other RTs inherit Base + monthly supplement.
RT supplement
Historical ADR difference between an RT and the baseRT, split by high / low season. E.g. Suite with Terrace Firenze May = +€70 (high), +€50 (low).
LMF
Last-Minute Factor — adjustment matrix DoW × pickup-window, applied as multiplier to the RMES price.
Channel vs Source
Channel = direct booking channel (Booking, Beddy, Expedia, …). Source = where the guest came from (country, OTA, direct, …).
OTA markup
The mark-up the OTA adds to the loaded price: Expedia 17%, Booking 13%, Airbnb/VRBO 10%. Used to compare across channels in Beddy space.
